GoHighLevel, the one system

Every customer, every conversation and every automation at BSUK lives in one place. GoHighLevel is that place, and this manual is the tour.

GoHighLevel, GHL from here on, runs the whole customer side of the business. A lead from a Facebook ad, a WhatsApp thread, a booked design call, an estimate, a 500 pound deposit: each is recorded in GHL against the same contact, and the automations read that record to decide what happens next.

👤
Contacts
One record per person: details, tags, notes and full history. Everything else in GHL hangs off it.
💬
Conversations
WhatsApp, SMS and email in a single inbox, so whoever picks up a thread sees the whole exchange.
📊
Pipelines
Boards of cards tracking where every deal stands. Read left to right, they are the state of the business.
📅
Calendars and automations
Design call bookings, and the workflows that send messages, move cards and raise alerts on their own.

Why one system matters
GHL is a CRM, a customer relationship management system. The whole point of a CRM is that every point of contact with a customer is held in one place: calls, WhatsApps, emails, notes, bookings and payments, all against the same record. That is why calling, messaging and note-taking happen inside GHL, one place, one complete history, and the automations act on it.
Everyone · Chapter 2 of 7

Wave 2, and why it exists

Wave 2 is our second round of Facebook and Meta ads. The name matters less than the story: Wave 1 proved the ads work, and taught us exactly what to build differently.

Wave 1 brought in real leads and real sales. It also ran on thin plumbing. Pipeline cards moved when somebody remembered to drag them, so the board often said one thing while reality said another.

The protection meant to stop automated messages reaching people mid-conversation depended on lists inside workflows, lists that had to name every other workflow and stay current forever. One was saved empty. Nothing turned red, it simply did nothing, and a customer who had spoken to the team by phone was chased by the system two days later as if that call had never happened. He told us exactly what he thought of it.

Wave 2 is the re-engineered answer: the same ads in front of the same audience, running on a system rebuilt around what Wave 1 taught us.

What Wave 1 taught usWhat Wave 2 does instead
Cards moved by hand, and the board drifted from realityEvery stage move fires off a recorded event: a form submitted, a call outcome, a booking, a payment
Suppression, holding automated messages back while a customer is mid-conversation with the team, relied on lists a person had to maintainEvery workflow checks for a live conversation before it sends, and stays quiet if it finds one
Triggers, the events that set an automation running, fired wider than intended and caught the wrong leadsTriggers are locked to the exact form, and every action names its pipeline explicitly
Why the history is in the manual
The rules in the coming chapters, never drag cards, never edit tags, exact spellings only, all exist because we watched what happens without them. Each rule is a repair, not caution for its own sake.

How Wave 2 thinks

Every Facebook lead travels the same road, and the system walks them down it. This chapter is the map, and the vocabulary for reading it.

Some terms first, they may be new. A pipeline is a board of columns, one column per stage of the journey from fresh enquiry to paid deposit. Each card on the board is one real person. When something meaningful happens to them, a call connects, a booking lands, a payment arrives, their card moves a column along. Reading the board left to right is reading the state of the business today. You will find it under Opportunities, named Wave 2 Facebook/Meta Ad.

A workflow is a recipe the system follows on its own: when this happens, do that. The "when" is called a trigger, the event that sets a workflow running: a form submitted, a call ending, a payment landing. Wave 2 is sixteen workflows working together. They send the first WhatsApp, place the calls, read what happened on each call, run the follow-up messages and move the cards. What they cannot do is talk to a customer. That part never stopped being yours.

Why the cards move themselves
A stage change is never just a label. Landing in a stage fires that stage's messages, and leaving one switches them off. The automation moves a card only once it has recorded the real event, a call outcome, a booking, a payment, so what a customer hears always matches what actually happened to them. That is also why nobody drags cards by hand: a hand-moved card can fire the wrong messages at a real person, or silence the right ones, with no error shown anywhere. The division of labour is simple. The system dials, routes and remembers. People do the talking and the persuading.

Tags, the wiring

Tags look like labels. At BSUK they are how the automation remembers, and how it decides.

Every workflow decision, send this, skip that, wait, alert someone, is the system reading a contact's tags and branching on exactly what it finds. Two consequences follow. Tags are matched by exact spelling, one changed letter and a workflow silently stops finding anyone. And the system never removes tags on its own, so a contact's tag list is also their history, which later workflows rely on to know what has already happened.

Why deleting is the dangerous one
A tag mistake is invisible. Nothing turns red, no error fires. A workflow quietly stops matching, a customer quietly stops hearing from us, and nobody finds out for weeks, usually when the customer calls to ask why we went silent. That is why the rule is blunt: never delete, never rename, add only when asked. It is not about trust, it is that the failure mode has no alarm.

The never-do list

Five things nobody does in GHL, whatever their role, even to fix something. Each one has an automation depending on it staying untouched.

🏷️

📈

⚙️

The workflows in there are the machinery under everything in this manual. Look freely, touch nothing.
📅

Booking flows, reminders and card moves all hang off the calendars being exactly as built.
📤
Never run a bulk action or mass send

💬
And on WhatsApp, mind the window
Outside 24 hours of the customer's last message, only an approved template actually delivers. Typed messages silently fail.

When something looks odd

A card in a strange place, a message that should have sent and did not, a contact whose story does not add up. Whoever spots it, the drill is the same.

1
📷 Screenshot it as found
Before anything changes. The state of the screen at that moment is usually the whole diagnosis.
2
📝 Note the contact, the time, and how you got there
Those three details let Nikki replay exactly what the system did and find the wiring behind it.
3
💬 WhatsApp Nikki, then hands off
Not because you could not fix it, but because a hand fix hides the evidence. The odd thing is a symptom, and it needs to stay intact so the cause gets cured, not the case.
When in doubt, send it
A false alarm costs thirty seconds. A real problem caught early can save a customer. There is no version of this where sending was the wrong call.

The system does the remembering, the chasing and the routing. People do the noticing, the judgement and the conversations.

Log in as you

Everyone at BSUK works in GHL under their own login, never a shared inbox account. Everything else in the Office chapters depends on it.

Here is the why. Every click in GHL is stamped with the logged-in user. On a shared login, the system cannot tell one person's work from another's: it cannot give a task to you by name, cannot credit a call or a note to you, and cannot notify you about your own contacts, because as far as GHL knows, you do not exist.

📋
Tasks can find you
The system can put your name on a task, and only what is genuinely yours shows in your list.
🧭
Work is routed properly
Some things need Liam's authority, pricing, refunds, product calls. Separate logins keep his list holding only those, and yours holding the rest.
📊
The record tells the truth
Who called, who noted, who booked. When something needs unpicking later, the history says who actually did it.
🔒
And it is safer
Your own password, and shared logins stop being handed around.
From today
Always your login, never a shared account. If you find a browser signed in as a shared inbox, log out and back in as yourself before doing anything. The one minute it takes keeps the record honest.
✍️ Prove it, thirty seconds

In GHL, click your avatar at the top right and look at the name on the account. Type that name here. The Got it tick below stays locked until you do, because this one matters more than any other rule in the Office chapters.

Tasks, the moments the system asks for you

Wave 2 was built to need people as little as possible. Chases, reminders, follow-ups all run on their own. When the system creates a task, it has hit something only a human can do.

A lead replied after the chase gave up, a call needs making, a judgement needs a person. WhatsApp templates and the 24 hour rule

WhatsApp only lets a business message a customer freely within 24 hours of the customer's last message. Outside that window, only a pre-approved template will actually arrive.

The trap is how it fails. A typed message outside the window does not bounce or warn you. It shows as sent on our side and simply never reaches them, so the customer thinks we ignored them and we think we replied. Templates exist because Meta requires the wording of that outreach to be approved in advance, and once approved, a template always delivers, any time.

1
In the conversation, check the channel and the clock
WhatsApp selected as the channel, and a look at when the customer last wrote. Inside 24 hours, type freely. Outside it, template only.
2
Open the template picker in the message toolbar
Pick the one that fits, fill in any blanks it asks for, and send.
3
Check it shows delivered, not just sent
Delivered means it reached their phone. Sent alone, on an outside-window typed message, is the silent failure this chapter exists to avoid.

Where templates come from
Templates are written in GHL and submitted to Meta for approval, which can take minutes or a day or two, so we keep a set ready. After the call, the system reads it

You do not file an outcome. The automation reads your transcript and files it for you, which is why the words you use on the call matter.

A few minutes after a call ends, the AI reads the transcript, writes a call summary into Notes, outcome, product discussed, objections, next touch, and sets a Call Outcome on the contact. Tags follow from that, and what the customer hears next runs off the tags.

✅
Confirmed
They are going ahead. Send the deposit link if you have not already, the next chapter covers it.
🤔
Thinking
Interested, not decided. The system keeps them warm. What they are weighing up, in their own words, is the note that makes the next touch useful.
❌
No
Not for them. Sequences stop, and nobody chases a no.
❓
Unclear
Nearly always means the call never really happened: a voicemail, a hang-up, a wrong number. A missed connection message goes out inviting them to reply, WhatsApp or book a callback.
Say it in plain words
The AI can only file what the transcript shows. Draw the answer out of the customer and say it back to them: so you are happy to go ahead, so you would like to think it over. A mumbled maybe cannot be filed, and a hopeful outcome books your own time against someone who will not show.

Call outcomes, and what each one triggers

Your outcome is a switch. The moment you record it, the system routes the lead down one of four roads, so the honest outcome is the helpful one.

🔥

❌
No

Unclear

Why honest beats hopeful
Marking a polite-but-cold lead as warm does not help anyone. It books sales team time against someone who will not show, and it sends the lead messages that feel pushy because they never asked for them. The system amplifies whatever you record.

Call Team · Chapter 5 of 5

When to hand a caller to BSUK

Some things are not for the first call to solve, and passing them on quickly is the professional move, not a failure.

1
Pricing beyond the published list, discounts, refunds
These need BSUK authority. Take the details, promise a call back from the team, record the outcome honestly.
2
Upset or complaining callers
Acknowledge, take details, do not defend or explain the company's side. A named person from BSUK will come back to them.
3
Never guess an answer
Build details, delivery timelines, technical specifics. A wrong answer travels further than a we will find out for you.
One name to remember
To customers, the founder is James. If a caller asks for the owner or mentions James, that is normal, route it through the handover as usual.

Every rule in this manual exists for the same reason. So the customer's experience is true to what actually happened to them.
