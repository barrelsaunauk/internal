Safety & longevity first

A sauna combines high temperatures, timber, electricity, moisture and, in some cases, solid-fuel heating.

That means the details matter.

Our philosophy is not:

“What is the minimum needed to build a sauna?”

It is:

“What can we do to build it properly, protect it properly and help it last?”

Many lower-cost and imported sauna packages are designed around reaching a particular price point.

Important protective features, weatherproofing measures or finishing details can sometimes be reduced, omitted or treated as extras.

We take a different approach.

Where appropriate, we build additional protection into the sauna from the beginning.

Substantial Thermowood construction

Our barrel saunas use substantial Thermowood construction, with approximately 48 mm barrel staves on our standard builds.

We want the structure itself to feel solid and substantial rather than simply using the minimum amount of timber needed.

The combination of good materials, correct construction, weather protection, ventilation and proper aftercare all contribute towards the long-term performance of the sauna.

Built with British weather in mind

An outdoor sauna in Britain has to deal with far more than heat on the inside.

It has to withstand:

  • Heavy rain
  • Persistent moisture
  • Wind
  • Temperature changes
  • Winter weather
  • Freeze-thaw cycles
  • UV exposure
  • Repeated expansion and contraction of timber

That is why longevity starts outside the sauna as well as inside it.

Depending on the project, we can add additional weather protection including:

  • Additional roof framing over the barrel
  • Secondary protective roof structures
  • Extra protection over the sauna shell
  • Improved water run-off
  • Additional timber treatments
  • Extra protection around exposed areas
  • Choice of roofing systems
  • Protective exterior finishes

A secondary roof above the barrel

For customers wanting additional long-term protection, we can construct a dedicated framed roof structure above the barrel itself.

This creates an additional layer between the main sauna shell and the weather.

Rather than relying solely on the barrel shell to absorb years of rain, wind and exposure, the secondary roof helps provide another level of protection.

It can also dramatically change the appearance of the sauna and make the finished build feel more architectural.
